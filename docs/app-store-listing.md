## App name and subtitle

These are the two highest weighted fields in App Store search, above the
keyword field. Apple indexes name, subtitle and keywords as one pool and
combines words across them automatically, so a word must never appear in two
of the three.

Recommended:

    Name      Revely: Food Label Scanner      (26 / 30)
    Subtitle  Allergy & Ingredient Checker    (28 / 30)

That indexes: revely, food, label, scanner, allergy, ingredient, checker.
Combined across fields it covers "food scanner", "food label scanner",
"ingredient scanner", "food allergy scanner", "allergy checker", "ingredient
checker", "label checker" and so on.

What changed and why:

* "See What's Inside" spends 17 of the 30 characters in the highest weighted
  field on words nobody searches for. It is the better line to read and the
  worse line to be found by.
* "Food" was missing from all three fields. It is the most common word in the
  queries this app should win, and it now sits in the name.
* "Scanner" moves from the subtitle to the name, which weights it higher, and
  the subtitle picks up "checker" so both verbs are covered.

Brand preserving alternative, if the name matters more than the ranking:

    Name      Revely: See What's Inside       (25 / 30)
    Subtitle  Food Label & Allergy Scanner    (28 / 30)

This keeps the line you wrote and still gets "food" indexed. It costs you
"ingredient" and "checker" in the weighted fields, and "ingredient" then has
to come out of the 100 character keyword budget at 10 characters.

Now is the free moment to change either one: name and subtitle can only be
edited with a version, and 1.0 has not shipped.

97 of 100 characters, with no word repeated from the name or subtitle. "Label"
came out because the name now carries it, which bought room for "sesame".

"Allergen" and "allergy" are deliberately both present, in different fields:
"food allergy" is the far more common query, so it belongs in the weighted
subtitle, while "allergen" catches the rest from the keyword field.

Swap candidates: nuts, shellfish, lactose, preservative, family, baby.

### Notes (4,000 char limit)

HOW TO SIGN IN

Revely has no passwords, so there is no demo account to hand over. Please sign in with Apple, using your own Apple Account or a sandbox account. The button is on the first screen and again on the subscription screen. Sign in with Google also works.

HOW TO GET PAST THE SUBSCRIPTION SCREEN

Every feature is behind a subscription, so the review needs a purchase to proceed. In the sandbox environment the purchase completes at no charge.

1. Open the app and work through the short setup. It asks who the profile is for: choose "Myself". Adding one allergy, for example peanuts, is enough.
2. On the subscription screen, choose either plan and tap Continue.
3. Tap Sign in with Apple in the sheet that appears. The purchase sheet follows on its own.
4. Confirm the purchase. Annual includes a 7-day free trial; monthly bills immediately. Both complete without a charge in the sandbox.
5. The camera opens.

"Restore purchases" is on the subscription screen, under the plans, beside Terms of Use and Privacy Policy.

HOW TO TEST THE APP ITSELF

Point the camera at the ingredient list on any packaged food, filling the frame with the list. A product containing the allergen entered during setup is the clearest test: the verdict names the ingredient it found, where it sits on the list, and the published source behind the finding.

If photographing a product is not practical, there is an option to type an ingredient list in by hand.

ACCOUNT DELETION

In-app account deletion is at Menu, then Account, then Delete account. It removes the account, its profiles and its scan history.

WHAT THIS APP IS NOT

Revely provides ingredient information only. It is not a medical device, does not diagnose or treat any condition, and is not a substitute for professional medical advice. That disclaimer appears on every result screen in the app.

Ingredient findings are quoted from published sources, namely the FDA, EFSA, IARC and the WHO, and each verdict names the source it came from rather than asserting a conclusion of our own.

CAMERA

The camera is used to read ingredient lists. The photo is used for that one scan and is not retained.
